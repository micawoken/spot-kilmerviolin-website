# CONTRIBUTION POLICY

## Preface
This repository for the "Diversifying the Violin Curriculum for Private Teaching" project is used primarily to host the project's website. However, the source code powering the website is released under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later, and several additional terms for the following reasons:
1. To allow future contributors to the project the ability to maintain the website, if its system administrator (Michael Wong) were to ever be unable to maintain it;
2. To allow anyone on the Internet to use and modify the source code for their own use, so long as they comply with the terms of the AGPL and the additional terms; and
3. To allow anyone on the Internet to study and inspect the source code, for any purpose.

This repository's administrators also recognize the following:
1. The use of machine learning systems (artificial intelligence, or AI, in common parlance) for software development and engineering, greatly expand the scope of individuals who are able to review and contribute to this project; but
2. Said machine learning systems produce code of questionable quality, and thus the onus of code review and verification is put solely on the human submitting it.

This contribution policy reflects these three interests.

## Who may contribute
The following groups may contribute to this project:
1. Members, current and former, of the "Diversifying the Violin Curriculum for Private Teaching" project, who are noted in the CONTRIBUTORS.md file; and
2. Any individual who can access the repository on the Internet.

## What may be contributed
Any individual who may contribute to this project may contribute the following:
1. Reporting of any issues - publicly to GitHub Issues, and privately to kilmer_security@mwmsc.net (for the official website);
2. Bug fixes for any legitimate purpose - security-related, design-related, compliance-related, or otherwise; and
3. Suggestions of features.

## How to contribute
### Issue reporting
As mentioned earlier, you may report issues either publicly on GitHub Issues, or privately to kilmer_security@mwmsc.net. When you report an issue, please include the following:
1. A description of the issue: in 1-2 sentences, what is wrong;
2. Intended behavior: what should the code be doing;
3. Actual behavior: what the code is doing;
4. Steps to reproduce: a series of clear steps a maintainer can execute to reproduce the issue

Issues that do not include all four above points will be rejected.

### Bug fixes
Bug fixes generally entail the suggestion of specific code to modify (which, for this policy, distinguishes it from issues).

When you propose a bug fix, include the following in your PR:
1. A description of the big: in 1-2 sentences, what is wrong;
2. Intended behavior: what should the code be doing;
3. Actual behavior: what the code is doing;
4. Steps to reproduce: a series of clear steps a maintainer can execute to reproduce the bug;
5. Your patch: explain, in 1-4 sentences, how your patch fixes the problem.

You also must comply with the AI-assisted code policy noted earlier.

### Suggestions
You are free to make suggestions to the project that may otherwise improve it. Since the maintainers are busy, keep your submission to 500 words or less. (Please also see the Conflict of Interest policy later.)

## Conditions of contribution
These conditions apply to all non-members of the project. However, compliance is encouraged for all contributors.

### AI policy
Specific requirements as it pertains to code are delineated later; this policy controls the text of any issue, fix, or suggestion you make.

It is encouraged that you write in your own voice - being able to articulate the problem or suggestion you want demonstrates to us that you acted in good faith. However, we will not immediately reject your submission if you use an LLM or translation service: we understand that not everyone comes from a technical background and that not everyone speaks English fluently. As such, we may apply a heightened level of scrutiny to submissions that appear to be generated by an LLM, but we will not reject it solely for being AI-generated.

#### AI-assisted code policy
The maintainers understand that AI coding tools can be vastly helpful in software development. However, the maintainers also have lives outside of this project, code from AI coding tools can often contain bugs and other hallucinations, and preserving our sanity and time is important to us. As such, the following policy applies to AI-generated code:
1. In your PR, you must indicate whether you used an AI coding tool to generate any portion of the code. If it is determined you failed to disclose use of an AI coding tool, your submission will be rejected. You must similarly identify the model used to generate the code. You can comply with this requirement by having your AI agent include the Co-Authored-By message in your Git commit, which must include the model name.
2. In your PR, if it contains AI-generated code, you must affirm that you have personally reviewed the entire output of the LLM. If you disclose use of an AI-generated coding tool, but fail to affirm you have personally reviewed it, your submission will be rejected.
3. If your submission is rejected on the basis of this policy, we may allow you to cure your submission after-the-fact within a time frame we determine. However, we are not obligated to review your suggestions, so we are under no obligation to do so.

### Code of conduct
This policy applies to all contributors, including members.

All contributors are expected to, very generally, "treat others the way they want to be treated." This includes, but is not limited to:
1. Treating other contributors with respect,
2. Offering criticism constructively, and
3. Using language appropriate for the forum.

We hold a zero-tolerance policy for harassment, bullying, and other disrespectful and non-courteous behavior. We may take all appropriate measures to prevent this.

### Conflict of interest policy
In any suggestion received by the maintainers, you may choose to identify a specific project, resource, or commercial product. If you fail to disclose any conflict of interest you may have between the resource you promote and yourself, your submission will be rejected, and, at our disrection, we may restrict your ability to contribute in the future.

We hold a zero-tolerance policy for individuals who make suggestions with a commercial or promotional interest in mind - this is not the space for shilling, and we reserve the right to take all appropriate measures to prevent that.
